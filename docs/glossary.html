Glossary

210 key terms from every lesson. Search, or follow a link to the lesson where the term is explained.

Alert
A warning from a security tool that something may be wrong. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Alert fatigue
When too many alerts make analysts miss the important ones. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Artefact
A trace left by normal use, such as browser history or a recently opened file. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Asymmetric encryption
Encryption that uses a public key to lock and a private key to unlock, such as RSA. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Authentication
Checking who you are. (Web Security Basics)
Authorisation
Checking what you are allowed to do. (Web Security Basics)
Authorisation
Permission, ideally in writing, from someone with the authority to give it. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Back end
The part that runs on the server and manages the data. (Web Security Basics)
Baiting
Offering something tempting, such as a USB drive or free download, to deliver malware. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
Black hat
A hacker who breaks in for personal gain or to cause harm. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Black-box test
A test where the tester is given almost no information in advance. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Blue team
The defenders who protect, detect and respond. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Blue team
The defenders who protect systems, detect attacks and respond to them. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Brute force
Trying every possible key until one works. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Brute force
Trying a large number of passwords or keys until one works. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Bug bounty
A programme that pays researchers for valid reports of flaws in defined systems. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Business email compromise
Impersonating a boss or supplier by email to get staff to pay money or change bank details. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
Capture the Flag
A puzzle-based security contest using safe, fictional targets. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Certificate
A signed document that links a website to its public key. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Chain of custody
The record of who handled evidence, when and where. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
chmod
The command that changes a file's permissions. (The Linux Command Line)
CIA triad
The three goals of security: confidentiality, integrity and availability. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Cipher
The method or algorithm used to encrypt and decrypt. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Ciphertext
The scrambled version of a message after encryption. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Client and server
A client asks for something; a server waits for requests and answers them. (How Networks Work)
Collision
Two different inputs that produce the same hash. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Computer Misuse Act 1990
The main UK law against unauthorised access to computers and damaging acts against them. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Containment
Stopping an incident from spreading. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Cookie
A small piece of data a browser stores and sends back to a site, often holding a session ID. (Web Security Basics)
Cryptography
The science of keeping information secret and proving it has not been changed. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
CSRF
Cross-site request forgery: tricking a logged-in browser into sending an unwanted request. (Web Security Basics)
CVSS
A scoring system that rates how severe a vulnerability is. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Cyber kill chain
A model of an attack as a series of stages from research to achieving the goal. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Data breach
When personal data is lost, stolen or exposed. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Data minimisation
Collecting only the personal data you actually need. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Data protection
Laws, such as UK GDPR, about how personal information may be collected and used. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Deepfake
A fake image, audio or video made with AI to look or sound real. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
Default credentials
The username and password a device ships with, which should always be changed. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Default deny
A firewall approach that blocks everything except what is explicitly allowed. (How Networks Work)
Defence in depth
Using several layers of protection, so that one failure is not fatal. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Defence in depth
Using several layers of protection. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Denial of service
Overloading a system so that real users cannot use it. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Developer tools
The built-in browser tools (F12) for inspecting a page, requests and cookies. (Web Security Basics)
DHCP
The service that automatically gives devices an IP address when they join a network. (How Networks Work)
Digital forensics
Recovering and analysing digital evidence in a way that can be relied on and explained. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Digital signature
A value made with a private key that proves who sent a message and that it is unchanged. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Distribution
A complete Linux system made by bundling the kernel with other software, such as Ubuntu or Kali. (The Linux Command Line)
DKIM
An email check using a digital signature added by the sending domain. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
DMARC
A policy that says what to do when SPF or DKIM checks fail. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
DNS
The Domain Name System: it turns names into IP addresses. (How Networks Work)
DNS record
Information that links a name to a server, which can show relationships between sites. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Doxxing
Publishing someone's private information to expose or harass them. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
EDR
Endpoint Detection and Response: software that watches computers for suspicious behaviour. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Encapsulation
Each layer wrapping data in its own envelope as it passes down the layers. (How Networks Work)
Encoding
Changing the format of data (such as Base64). It has no key and gives no secrecy. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Encryption
Scrambling data so that only someone with the right key can read it. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Enumeration
Finding out which services and versions are running, and what they reveal. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Eradication
Removing the cause of an incident, such as malware or a bad account. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Ethical hacker
A security professional who works with permission to find and help fix weaknesses. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Ethical hacker
A security professional who tests systems with permission. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
EXIF
The standard format for photo metadata, which can include camera, time and GPS location. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Exploit
A method of using a vulnerability to do something unintended. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
False positive
An alert that flags something harmless. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
File carving
Recovering files from raw disk data by recognising their signatures. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
File signature
A fixed pattern of bytes at the start of a file that shows its true type, also called a magic number. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Firewall
A device or program that allows or blocks traffic using rules. (How Networks Work)
Footprint
The trail of information about you or an organisation that exists online. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Forensic image
An exact, bit-for-bit copy of a drive or device, used so the original is never altered. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Frequency analysis
Breaking a cipher by counting how often letters appear. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Front end
The part of a web app that runs in the browser: HTML, CSS and JavaScript. (Web Security Basics)
Geolocation
Working out where a photo or video was taken from its visible clues. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Geotag
Location data attached to a photo or post. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Grey hat
A hacker who acts without permission but with no harmful aim, which is still unlawful. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Hash
A fixed-length fingerprint of data, used to prove that a copy is identical. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Hashing
Turning any input into a fixed-length, one-way fingerprint. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Header
Extra information sent with a request or response, such as cookies or the server software. (Web Security Basics)
Header (email)
Hidden lines in an email that record its journey and authentication checks. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Hex viewer
A tool that shows the raw bytes of a file. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Hexadecimal
A base-16 number system often used to show raw bytes. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Hidden file
A file whose name starts with a dot, which a plain ls does not show. (The Linux Command Line)
Hop
One router that a packet passes through on its journey. (How Networks Work)
HTTP
The protocol browsers and servers use to request and send web pages. (Web Security Basics)
HTTPS
HTTP carried inside an encrypted connection. (Web Security Basics)
ICO
The Information Commissioner's Office, which regulates data protection in the UK. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
IDOR
Insecure Direct Object Reference: reaching someone else's data by changing an ID the server does not check. (Web Security Basics)
IDS / IPS
Intrusion Detection System (alerts) and Intrusion Prevention System (alerts and blocks). (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Incident response
The organised process of dealing with a security incident. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Indicator of compromise
A clue that a system may have been breached. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Information disclosure
A system revealing details, such as software versions, that help attackers. (Web Security Basics)
IP address
An address that identifies a device on a network and is used to route data between networks. (How Networks Work)
Kerckhoffs's principle
A system should stay secure even if everything except the key is public. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Key
The secret value that controls how a cipher scrambles a message. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Key exchange
A method, such as Diffie–Hellman, for two parties to agree a shared secret over an open line. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
LAN
Local Area Network: the devices in one home, school or office. (How Networks Work)
Lateral movement
Moving from one compromised machine to others on the same network. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Least privilege
Giving users and programs only the access they need. (The Linux Command Line)
Least privilege
Giving each part of a system only the access it needs. (Web Security Basics)
Linux
An open-source operating system kernel, and the family of systems built around it. (The Linux Command Line)
Log
A file that records events, such as logins, usually in /var/log. (The Linux Command Line)
Log
A record of events on a system, with timestamps. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Lookalike domain
A web or email address that closely resembles a real one. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
LSB
Least significant bit: the final bit of a number, which can be changed invisibly to hide data in an image. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
MAC address
A 48-bit hardware address built into a network card, used for delivery inside one local network. (How Networks Work)
Metadata
Data about data, such as when and where a photo was taken. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Misconfiguration
A system set up in an insecure way, such as an unneeded service left switched on. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
MITRE ATT&CK
A public catalogue of real attacker techniques, used to check defences. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Multi-factor authentication
Logging in with more than one proof of identity, such as a password and a code. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
NAT
Network Address Translation: a router swapping private addresses for its public address and back. (How Networks Work)
Network
Two or more devices connected so they can share data. (How Networks Work)
Nmap
A popular tool that finds devices, open ports and service versions on a network. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Octet
One group of 8 bits in an IPv4 address, shown as a number from 0 to 255. (How Networks Work)
One-time pad
An unbreakable cipher that uses a random key as long as the message, used only once. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Order of volatility
The principle of collecting the most fragile evidence first. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
OSINT
Open-source intelligence: information gathered, checked and connected from public sources. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Output encoding
Converting special characters to harmless text before showing user input in a page. (Web Security Basics)
OWASP Top 10
A regularly updated list of the most serious web application security risks. (Web Security Basics)
Owner
The user who owns a file and has the first set of permissions. (The Linux Command Line)
Packet
A small piece of data sent across a network, with addressing information attached. (How Networks Work)
Packet capture
A recording of network traffic, often a .pcap file. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Parameterised query
A query where data is sent separately from the command, which prevents SQL injection. (Web Security Basics)
Passive collection
Looking only at information that is already public, without interacting with the target. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Password manager
A tool that stores a different strong password for every account. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
Path
The address of a file or folder. It can be absolute (starts with /) or relative. (The Linux Command Line)
Penetration test
A security test carried out with the owner's permission to find weaknesses. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Penetration test
A permitted, human-led attempt to find and use weaknesses in a system, in order to improve it. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Permissions
Settings that say who may read, write or execute a file. (The Linux Command Line)
Persistence
A method an attacker uses to keep access after a restart or logout. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Personal data
Information about an identifiable living person, such as a name, address or photo. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Phishing
A fraudulent message that pretends to be from a trusted source to get you to click, open or reveal something. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
Pipe
The | symbol, which passes the output of one command to the next. (The Linux Command Line)
Pivot
Using one piece of information to find another. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Plaintext
The original, readable message. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Playbook
A step-by-step procedure for handling a type of alert or incident. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Port
A number that identifies a particular service on a computer, from 0 to 65535. (How Networks Work)
Pretext
A made-up scenario used to make a request seem believable. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
Pretexting
Inventing a believable scenario to extract information. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
Privacy settings
Controls that decide who can see your accounts and posts. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Private address
An IP address in a reserved range that is used inside homes and offices and is not routed on the internet. (How Networks Work)
Private key
The half of a key pair that must be kept secret. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Privilege escalation
Gaining higher access than you were given, such as moving from an ordinary user to root. (The Linux Command Line)
Privilege escalation
Gaining more access than you started with. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Prompt
The text shown before your cursor, such as student@trainer:~$. (The Linux Command Line)
Protocol
An agreed set of rules for how two computers talk to each other. (How Networks Work)
Public key
The half of a key pair that can be shared with anyone. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Purple team
Red and blue teams working together. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Quishing
Phishing that uses a malicious QR code. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
Ransomware
Malware that encrypts files and demands payment to restore them. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Reconnaissance
Gathering information about a target before testing it. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Red team
A group that simulates a real attacker. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Redirect
Sending output into a file with > (replace) or >> (add to the end). (The Linux Command Line)
Remediation
Fixing a problem found in a test. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Request and response
The browser sends a request; the server sends back a response with a status code. (Web Security Basics)
Responsible disclosure
Reporting a security problem privately and giving the owner time to fix it. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Reverse image search
Searching with a picture to find where else it appears. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Root (account)
The all-powerful administrator account on a Linux system. (The Linux Command Line)
Root (directory)
The top of the Linux file system, written /. (The Linux Command Line)
Router
A device that joins networks and forwards packets using IP addresses. (How Networks Work)
Rules of engagement
The agreed rules for a test: times, methods, limits and contacts. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Salt
A random value added to a password before hashing so identical passwords have different hashes. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Scope
The exact list of systems and actions that a test is allowed to cover. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Script
A text file containing a list of commands that run together. (The Linux Command Line)
Search operator
A symbol or keyword, such as site: or filetype:, that narrows a search. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Security clearance
A background check needed for some sensitive jobs, which a criminal record can affect. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Segmentation
Dividing a network into separate zones to limit the spread of an attack. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Session
The period during which the server recognises you as logged in. (Web Security Basics)
Shell
The program that reads your commands and runs them. The most common one is bash. (The Linux Command Line)
Shoulder surfing
Watching someone enter a password or read a screen. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
SIEM
Security Information and Event Management: a system that collects and analyses logs and raises alerts. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
SIFT
A method for checking information: Stop, Investigate the source, Find better coverage, Trace claims to the origin. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Smishing
Phishing by SMS or messaging apps. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
SOC
Security Operations Centre: a team that monitors an organisation's systems and responds to threats. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
Social engineering
Manipulating people into giving information or access. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
Social proof
The pull of doing what other people seem to be doing. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
Spear phishing
Phishing aimed at one specific person or organisation, using real details about them. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
SPF
An email check that the sending server is allowed to send for the domain. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
SPF, DKIM and DMARC
Email standards that help receivers check that a message really came from the domain it claims. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
SQL
Structured Query Language, the language used to talk to most databases. (Web Security Basics)
SQL injection
Tricking a website into running attacker-supplied SQL by putting it in an input. (Web Security Basics)
Status code
A number in a response that says what happened, such as 200 (OK) or 404 (not found). (Web Security Basics)
Steganography
Hiding the existence of a message inside another file. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Subdomain
A prefix on a web address, such as the word before the main domain. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
Subnet mask
A number (such as 255.255.255.0 or /24) that shows which part of an address is the network and which is the device. (How Networks Work)
sudo
A command that runs one other command with administrator rights. (The Linux Command Line)
Switch
A device that connects devices in one network and forwards data using MAC addresses. (How Networks Work)
Symmetric encryption
Encryption that uses the same key to lock and unlock, such as AES. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
Tailgating
Following an authorised person through a secure door. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
TCP
A reliable protocol that checks every packet arrives, in order. (How Networks Work)
Terminal
The window where you type commands. (The Linux Command Line)
Three-way handshake
The SYN, SYN-ACK, ACK exchange that sets up a TCP connection. (How Networks Work)
Timeline
Events from several sources arranged in time order to show what happened. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Triage
Sorting alerts by seriousness and likelihood so the most important are handled first. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
True positive
An alert that correctly identifies a real problem. (Blue Team: Defending and Responding)
UDP
A fast, lightweight protocol that does not check delivery. (How Networks Work)
UK GDPR
The UK's data protection regulation, working alongside the Data Protection Act 2018. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
Unauthorised access
Getting into a system or its data without the permission of the person entitled to give it. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
UTC
Coordinated Universal Time, the standard used to compare timestamps from different places. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Verification
Checking that a piece of information is true, current and from a reliable source. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Vishing
Phishing by voice call. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
Vulnerability
A weakness that could be misused. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Vulnerability scan
An automatic check that lists possible weaknesses. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
Web archive
A service that stores old copies of web pages. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Whaling
Spear phishing aimed at senior people. (Social Engineering: Hacking People)
White hat
A hacker who works with permission to improve security. (Law, Ethics and Privacy)
White-box test
A test where the tester is given full details of the system. (Penetration Testing: Thinking Like an Attacker)
WHOIS
A public lookup that shows registration details for a domain name. (OSINT: Investigating with Open Sources)
Wildcard
A symbol such as * that matches any text, as in *.txt. (The Linux Command Line)
Wireshark
A free tool for viewing and analysing packet captures. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
Write blocker
A device that lets data be read from a drive but never written to it. (Digital Forensics: Following the Evidence)
XOR
A bit operation that outputs 1 when the two bits differ. Doing it twice with the same key restores the original. (Cryptography: Secrets and Proof)
XSS
Cross-site scripting: getting a site to run an attacker's script in other visitors' browsers. (Web Security Basics)
